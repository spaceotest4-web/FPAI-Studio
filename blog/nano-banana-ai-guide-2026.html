"Nano Banana" is one of the most searched AI terms of 2026 — a playful codename for a serious model. It is the engine behind Google Gemini's image generation and editing, and it earned its reputation on one thing in particular: editing a photo you already have by just telling it what to change. This guide explains what Nano Banana actually is, how to use it, what the Nano Banana 2 upgrade changed, where it struggles, and a genuinely free alternative that needs no Google account and adds AI video.

The short version: Nano Banana is Gemini's image model — excellent at conversational photo edits and reference-based generation, free inside the Gemini app with a Google account and daily limits. If you want image generation with no account at all, or you also need AI video, Aichance's free generator is the practical companion.

What is Nano Banana?

Nano Banana is the internal name for Google's native image model inside Gemini. Whenever you generate or edit an image in the Gemini app, Google AI Studio, the Gemini API, or Vertex AI, Nano Banana is the model producing the result. It does three things well: text-to-image generation, conversational editing of an uploaded photo ("remove the background", "make it night time", "add a red jacket"), and reference-guided generation, where you attach an image and it produces new variants tuned to it.

The name went viral because the editing felt different from typing a fresh prompt each time — you have a conversation with your own photo, and the model keeps the subject consistent while changing only what you asked. That character consistency across edits is what pushed it into mainstream searches.

How to use Nano Banana (step by step)

The easiest route is the Gemini app, which requires a free Google account:

  1. Open the Gemini app (or gemini.google.com) and sign in with your Google account.
  2. Tap the plus (+) icon next to the prompt box and choose Create image from the tools.
  3. Either pick a template from the gallery or type a description — for example, "a cozy coffee shop interior, warm morning light, photorealistic".
  4. Gemini returns a grid of variants. Hover or tap to download, open one in the built-in canvas to edit, or send a follow-up prompt to iterate.
  5. To edit your own photo, upload it and describe the change in plain language — Nano Banana applies it while keeping the rest of the image intact.

Developers can also reach the same model through Google AI Studio and the Gemini API, where generation is billed per image rather than capped by the app's daily free quota.

What Nano Banana 2 adds

Nano Banana 2 is the upgraded model Gemini now uses for its newest image features. The headline improvements are sharper photorealism, noticeably better text rendering inside images (long a weak spot for every AI generator), and stronger reference edits — attach a photo to a follow-up and it generates new variants matched to that reference. If accurate lettering on a poster, logo, or sign matters to you, Nano Banana 2 is a real step up over the first version.

What Nano Banana is great at

Credit where it is due — Nano Banana is one of the best tools available for a specific job:

  • Conversational photo editing — change one thing about a real photo without touching the rest, in plain language.
  • Subject consistency — the same person or object stays recognizable across multiple edits.
  • Reference-based generation — upload an image and get on-brand variants.
  • Text in images — with Nano Banana 2, legible words on posters and graphics are far more reliable than before.
  • Integration — it is right inside Gemini and Google Workspace apps like Slides.

Where Nano Banana falls short

No tool is the whole answer, and Nano Banana has clear boundaries worth knowing before you rely on it:

  • A Google account is required — there is no "just type and go" without signing in.
  • Daily free limits — heavy use in the app hits a quota; higher caps need a paid Google plan, and API use is billed per image.
  • No AI video in the image flow itself — Google's video generation is a separate product, not part of the Nano Banana image experience.
  • Less granular control — you do not choose between multiple image models or lock a seed for exact repeatability the way dedicated creative tools allow.

You can use Nano Banana for free inside the Gemini app with a Google account, subject to daily usage limits that are higher on paid Google AI plans. Using it through the Gemini API or Vertex AI is billed per image. So it is free for casual use but tied to a Google account and capped by quota.
